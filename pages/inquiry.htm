"All The News That May or May Not Be Fit To Print"

Here's a quote for you regarding casino gambling in proximity to Thoroughbred racing:

"This can be devastating to the horse racing industry.  Thoroughbred racing and casino gambling have never in the history of the United States been able to coexist at a profit for racing."

No, that didn't come from anyone at Woodbine, it was credited to Stormy Bidwell, then president of Sportsman's Park in Chicago, in Dave Surico's Sportsman's Park column in  the April 30-May 6, 1992 issue of the old Thoroughbred Racing Action newspaper.

In that now 20 year old column Surico went on to note that "Las Vegas had racing twice, and it went bankrupt twice.  They even tried dog racing and they went bankrupt.  The only way Atlantic City is making money now is by simulcasting New York races into Atlantic City.  Their actual live racing has been a disaster ever since the casinos went up.

"Where Indian gambling has been established in close proximity to racing, the races have deteriorated."

For those who think casinos are racing's salvation, bear in mind that people are going to find slot machines a far easier way to gamble with their discretionary income than handicapping.  And for those who say the slice of the casino pie handed over to racing more than compensates for the reduction in handle and boosts purses, there is neither a guarantee that casinos will forever subsidize racing while making it a second class attraction.

A Pox (or a dose of frog juice) on Sally Jenkins of the Washington Post, who was quoted in a recent Thoroughbred Times TODAY item:

"Thoroughbred racing is at a moral juncture, and it's time to decide whether it has any real worth, or needs to be outlawed."

And you just thought prohibition was a thing of the past...

The recent Royal Ascot meeting included a "new dress code" for patrons visiting the "Royal Enclosure and the Grandstand Admission area."

To insure that everyone abides by the new rules, Ascot had "a specially-trained team of dress code assistants" who would be stationed outside the course "to assist racegoers who may have any issues with their attire."

Don't you think all tracks should have a specially-trained team of dress code assistants lurking at the entrances?  I addressed this subject about 30 years ago in a column that is reprinted herein.  Go to On The Lite Side, "But You Gotta Wear A Tie," for more thoughts on this burning issue.

The Name Game:  You've gotta like the name of recent Bold Ruckus stakes winner Jenna's Wabbit, who was recently referred to by Woodbine's Keith McCalmont as "wascally" Jenna's Wabbit.

If you don't get that you've led a life void of Looney Tunes and Bugs Bunny and you have my deepest sympathy.
