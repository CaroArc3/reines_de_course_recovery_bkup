From time to time, a family we have in the “to do” hopper for the Reine-de-Course list makes us stop and say, “Okay, we need to do her now.”

Mirawala did just that at the end of 2001.

This daughter of Phalaris, bred by Major Giles Loder, had already accounted for such horses as Kentucky Derby winner Foolish Pleasure, top sire Tehran, G1 winner and useful sire Bagdad, and a handful of lesser horses. But all of a sudden in 2001, she began to make noise again.

Her descendent Valid Expectations became a top-class juvenile sire; Boreal won the German Derby; Tempera won the Breeders’ Cup Juvenile Fillies and became champion of her age and sex in the U. S. and the stallion Comet Shine, who has been moved more around more than a Law And Order rerun, began to show his worth. Not only is that a great deal of activity in a family in a single year, but consider how versatile and varied that activity is!

A word about Mirawala’s dam, Miranda, is in order.  She is the dam of Irish Derby winner King John and is the youngest sister of the hugely influential Reine-de-Course Pretty Polly, ancestress of Nearctic, Birgadier Gerard and so many others, as well as one of the greatest racemares of all time. Their dam, Admiration, was so poorly regarded that she once was sold for $35, one of her foals for $30.

As long as stories like this one are a part of Thoroughbred history, no man with a nice mare and a dream is going to give up. And maybe that is as it should be.  For all the common sense inherent in making money in this business, few of us ever approach the success of an Ashford/Coolmore. And those John Henrys and Free Houses just keep on coming, don’t they?  Maybe somebody’s looking out for the little guy after all.  The same somebody who awoke the family of Admiration all those many years ago and who seems to have given it a new wake up call at the advent of the twenty-first century.

New Reines-de-Course are Miranda; Mirawala, Becti, Bura, Bibi Sahiba and Stafaralla.  Their inclusion on the list is long overdue.
