May 1, 2004 - Regarding our April 14th article (below, which we have kept here for informational purposes) about Smarty Jones, and a pedigree that we found could definitely get a mile and a quarter, need we say more?

Smarty Jones

If Pennsylvania-bred Smarty Jones does not stay 10 furlongs, it is not the fault of his pedigree. The unbeaten colt, whose lineage has been roundly criticized by people who do not know how to read pedigrees, owns the following pedigree patterns:

Five lines of Frizette via Frizeur x2/Princess Palatine and Banshee x2.  He is inbred and tail-female to *La Troienne via Big Hurry/Baby League.  Also in his pedigree are three lines of Lavendula (*Source Sucree x2/*Ambiorix) and a three-way cross of three-quarter siblings Menow x2/Athenia.  Finally, his sire is inbred to half siblings Secretariat and Sir Gaylord.

There is no lack of recognizable classic names in his pedigree:  Kentucky Derby winners Secretariat, Northern Dancer and Foolish Pleasure all appear.  Preakness winner Bold Ruler is doubled.  English Derby winner Sir Ivor and French Derby winner *Herbager are also present.

So far as we are concerned, that is ample ammunition to get 10 furlongs.  All he needs now is what every Derby entrant needs - a major dose of Lady Luck.

REINE-DE-COURSE INFLUENCE IN

THE AMERICAN CLASSICS SINCE 1972

The tables for the three American classics illustrate which sires, dams and broodmare sires trace to Reine-de-Course families. Though the first, second, third, etc. dam may also be a Reine-de-Course the modern taproot mare (i.e. *Boudoir II with His Majesty) is used for the sake of consistency.

It should never be forgotten that the horse’s own tail-female line (i.e . his dam, second dam, third dam and so forth) is the single most important influence in his pedigree. The next most important is inbreeding and linebreeding to important ancestors.

In the future, we will also have a table illustrating the inbreeding and linebreeding in these classic winners.

Where a sire’s tail-female line is concerned, one wants to see a family that consistently produces good stallions (Mumtaz Mahal, *Uvira II, etc.) and where a broodmare sire is concerned, one is looking for an important individual who stood at a major stud farm.

No good broodmare sire ever stood at anything other than a major farm, as the definition of a good broodmare sire is a stallion who was bred to mares of good family.

REINES IN 2003 CLASSICS

Reines-de-Course continued to make a heavy impact on the 2003 classics in early June 2003. Descendents of named mares swept the Belmont and French Derby: Empire Maker (Teresina); Ten Most Wanted (*La Troienne) and Funny Cide, Dalakhani (St. Lucre); Super Celebre (Schwarzblaurot); and Coroner (*La Troienne).

Mumtaz Mahal, one of the first Reines-de-Course named also made her presence felt. Her descendent Yesterday won the Irish One Thousand Guineas and placed second in the Epsom Oaks, while her male descendent Alamshar ran third in the Epsom Derby. Second place Epsom Derby finisher The Great Gatsby, incidentally, traces to Heavenly Body (his fourth dam), a full sister to Reine-de-Course Hidden Talent.

Finally, Reine-de-Course Catnip is the tail-female descendent of Next Gina, winner of the German Oaks. Catnip is best-known as the second dam of Nearco, but she has a hugely important branch in Germany via Nella da Gubbio.
