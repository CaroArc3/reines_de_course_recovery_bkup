Boola Brook 

 

Filling Out The Family Line

When we originally began this series back in 1991, we had no idea that all these years later we’d still be writing about great mares and putting together all the pieces that make up great pedigrees.  It has, in fact, become our life’s work and we are grateful to have been blessed with the ability to do it.

Thus, what we are doing with the addition of Boola Brook to the Reine-de-Course list is completing the list of superior descendents of the mare *Belle Rose.  We’ve simply never tied it altogether before.

 

Background

The mare *Belle Rose (1889 by Beaudesert-Monte Rose by Craig Millar) was imported from England by James R. Keene’s Foxhall Stud.   She was one of many fine mares listed by Abram S. Hewitt in his Great Breeders And Their Methods, along with *Cinderella, *Mavoureen, *Sylvabelle and *Bonnie Doon.  This group of mares was responsible for the foundation upon which the early American Thoroughbred was built.  But we digress, for our subject is *Belle Rose:

 

The Great Daughter Branches

From her daughter Royal Rose by Royal Hampton came the Futurity winner Pennant, sire of the great Equipoise, and the Peter Pan mare Cherokee Rose II, which is responsible in direct descent for two important Reine-de-Course families:  Rare Bloom (Storm Cat, Royal Academy, etc.) and Erin (Ruffian, Fusaichi Pegasus, etc.)

Then, from *Belle Rose’s daughter Pink Domino came Belmont winner Sweep, broodmare sire of War Admiral, and his half sister Curioisty by Voter.   From Curiosity descends Reine-de-Course Wonder (John Henry, Hennessy, etc.)  We now come to the last important grouping of mares under this noble matron that has not been named, Boola Brook, whose fourth dam is *Belle Rose.

Boola Brook, a 1937 daughter of *Bull Dog, descends from Sweepaway, a half sister to Sweep and Curiosity by Wild Mint.   Her dam, Brookdale is by Peter Pan.  Thus this whole grouping of mares are rather closely related.

 

Matching Like to Like

As Rare Bloom and Bold Irish share a *Sir Gallahad III/Broomstick/Cherokee Rose II nexus, so do Brookdale and Wonder share a Domino/Hermit/Pink Domino cross.

Likewise, when one looks at our new Reine-de-Course Boola Brook and we compare her to Storm Cat’s fifth dam, Rare Bloom, we see a *Sir Gallahad III/Peter Pan/Belle Rose cross.  Freely translated, it’s all in the family.  So the next time some wise guy tells you John Henry is badly bred, remind him he’s from the same tail-female line that gave us Storm Cat.

 

The Perfect Opportunity

Even if she had not produced anything else, Boola Brook is important not just as another branch of *Belle Rose, but because she gave us Red God, tail-male ancestor of Blushing Groom, and Crafty Admiral, broodmare sire of Affirmed and sire of Admiral’s Voyage, broodmare sire of Danzig. 

But that is not all she did.  Also still out there, though admittedly not in huge numbers are crosses of horses like Beau Purple (especially via Exhuberant) and the mare Constant Nymph.  To top things off, she even gave us a Grand National winner in Hedgehunter (IRE), who withstood the rigors of the great steeplechase in 2005 after a terrifying fall in the same even in 2004.  We always did think ‘chasers are our bravest Thoroughbreds; and the National tends to bring out the best of that courage.  However, his is not to carry on for like most hurdlers, he is a gelding.

 

Speed, Speed and Speed

We’ve all seen that Wayne Lukas comment that the three most important things in a sire are speed, speed and speed.  Boola Brook’s grandson Red God met that criteria and then some.

By *Nasrullah and out of out Boola Brook’s unraced Menow daughter Spring Run, Red God was born the same year as Bold Ruler, *Gallant Man, Round Table and Gen. Duke.   Foaled at Claiborne like Round Table and Bold Ruler, Red God was bred by Cain Hoy Stable and was originally sent to run in England.

When he did not prosper under those conditions he was returned to his homeland and turned over to Cain Hoy’s conditioner, Woody Stephens.  Physical problems kept him from keeping his appointments in the classics and probably kept him from ever showing how good he was.  As a result, he was sent to Europe to stud and pretty well had to fend for himself, as he had no great racing reputation.  Plus he had never won at more than 8 ½ furlongs and did not appear to be the kind of classic sire Europeans preferred.

 

Doing It On His Own

Today, Red God’s branch of the *Nasrullah line is a strong one, and one of the few aside from Boldnesian via Seattle Slew that has prospered in the Northern Dancer/Raise A Native environment in which it finds itself. 

Thanks largely to Blushing Groom, there are strong branches via Runaway Groom-Cherokee Run and Mt. Livermore-Peaks and Valleys and young champion Orientate.  Arazi may even be redeemed via Congaree.  And of course while Rahy needs a good son to carry on both Fantastic Light and Bop hold promise.

There is also still a bit of Yellow God blood left out there, generally via Nebbiolo (appearing in the pedigrees of Selkirk and Compton Place), some Stanford (Paolini) and even some Green God (via Creaking Board in  young Maryland sire Crowd Pleaser).   Not to mention any number of daughter lines to be found here and there.

 

Crafty Admiral

If versatility is what one is searching for in the Thoroughbred, he need look no further than Crafty Admiral.   With a less potent male line (*Sir Gallahad III) than Red God’s *Nasrullah, he nonetheless had a somewhat better pedigree.

Crafty Admiral was inbred 2 x 3 to full brothers *Sir Gallahad III and *Bull Dog and his dam carried a double of Pink Domino via Sweep and his tail-female line of Sweepaway.   His overall lineage was a wondrous combination of speed from Domino and Bend Or and of stamina from St. Simon.

All this, Crafty Admiral put to good use and as one of only three foals produced by his dam, he made her immortal nonetheless with 28 on-the-board finishes in 39 starts and wins in such important handicaps as the Brooklyn, Gulfstream (twice) and New York and placings in the Suburban, Carter and Jockey Club Gold Cup.

He was champion handicapper at age five and, like Red God, was bred by the Cain Hoy Stable of Captain Harry F. Guggenehim.   Sold first at a Guggenheim dispersal as a yearling and later privately to the small stable of Charles Cohen, Crafty Admiral seemed determined to reward his new owners from saving him exile to a foreign land, as South American interests were said to fancy him.

Like Round Table a few years later, Crafty Admiral moved smoothly from track to track and from route to sprint.  He had no turf over which to prove himself, but found off going very much to his liking.

 

One From The Top And The Other From The Bottom

As Red God’s *Nasrullah-line influence helped him to keep Boola Brook’s line alive in tail-male, so did Crafty Admiral’s Teddy inbreeding make him destined to become better as a sire of good females.   Crafty Admiral has also become synonymous with toughness, giving horses like Affirmed and Crafty Prospector (his third dam, Crafty Princess, is by Crafty Admiral) more toughness than anyone had a right to expect from Raise A Native-line horses.

Other good daughters of Crafty Admiral were Our Jackie, dam of Our Native who ran third behind Alydar and Affirmed in the 1978 Derby and Preakness.  Then there was Banning, the dam of Grand Prix de Dauville (G2) winner *Balompie and Chem, winner of the Laurel Turf Cup and Tasma, dam of Arkansas Derby winner No Le Hace and Oaklawn Handicap winner Soy Numero Uno. 

In addition, the family which produced Crafty Prospector had quite a love affair with Crafty Admiral.  The mare *Adjournment by *Court Martial-Black Day by Foxhunter produced three daughters by Crafty Admiral. 

The first, Our Kretchen, became the second dam of Preakness winner Master Derby.  The next was the grand Frimanaha who won the Diana and Vineland Handicaps and placed in races like the Test, Molly Pitcher and Santa Margarita.  At stud, Frimanaha produced classic placed Bohemian Grove by Personality, who ran third in the Irish Derby and the stakes placed Bold Bidder colt Mateor who went to stud in Washington state. 

Danzig’s broodmare sire Admiral’s Voyage, by the way, won 12 of 52 starts and was versatile enough to sprint winning the Carter and stretch out to win the Wood Memorial and run second in the Belmont.  Obviously a horse with Fred Hooper connections, Admiral’s Voyage had siblings by *Quibu, Hoop Jr. and *My Babu.

 

The Pedigree of Boola Brook

What sets Boola Brook apart from most of the mares in her family is that she owns a rare cross of half siblings Minting and Mintcake, a cross one almost never sees.  Her sire *Bull Dog, of course, was inbred to St. Simon and his full sister Angelica and further back there is enough linebreeding to horses like Pocahontas (1837) and Lexington to make one appreciate the history he is really beholding.  This was a special mare, which is why her blood is still alive through two such outstanding sources.

 

And The New Reines Are

Filling out the family line, we here name Bell Rose, Pink Domino, Boola Brook, Admiral’s Lady and Spring Run to the Reine-de-Course list.  Remember how closely related some of the horses from this family are to previously-named Reines-de-Course like Rowes Bud, Wonder and Erin and use the knowledge wisely.  These great gifts from the old Keene stud were meant to meet again.
