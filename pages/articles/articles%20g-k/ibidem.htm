Ibidim

 

“King’s Best is bred altogether differently than Bluemamba.  He traces to the German foundation mare Ibidem via the Arabis group of mares whose family is responsible for a whole slew of German Derby winners:  Allgau; Athenagoras; Alba; Allasch; Asterblute; Ako; Abendfrieden; and Alchimist.  Arabis is certainly worthy of Reine status.  It’s gathering the information on German horses that is holding us up, not any failing on her part.”.......PEDLINES, June 2000.

 

            Of all the prophetic things we’ve written in these pages (and we’ve had our share), we’ve probably never hit the nail on the head quite so correctly as we did in this June 2000 story on European classic horses.  Since that time, dig as we might, the only information on Ibidem forthcoming was a list of her descendents and the name of her breeder (Gestuet Romerhof, breeder also of her dam, Bayreuth).

            We put her file aside, but did not forget her, despite one European friend assuring us that the family was getting weaker not stronger.  You know who you are, and you get our wet noodle award for that call!

            In any event, this year not only is the Ibidem family in the news, it literally IS the news in Europe.  Pacemaker wrote “Derby Winners Linked By German Roots”, and The Thoroughbred Times’ excellent pedigree writer John Sparkman wrote a whole story on Ibidem’s descendent Allegretta.  Thanks to that story, we can fill in the blank spots pertaining to one part of Ibidem’s family, though it does not tell the whole tale.

            We already knew that Ibidem had been sold by her breeder to Freiherr S. A. von Oppenheim, owner of Gestuet Schenderhan.  It was from this German stud that Allegretta, the subject of The Thoroughbred Times story, was purchased.

            What has caused this flurry of interest in Ibidem’s family (and most particularly in Allegretta’s sub-branch) is that since Allegretta herself produced 2000 Guineas winner King’s Best, not one but two of her daughters has produced a European Derby winner - and both of them accomplished that feat this year.  Allegretta’s Prix de l’Arc de Triomphe-winning daughter Urban Sea has out the English and Irish Derby winner Galileo and Allegretta’s stakes winning Riverman daughter Allez Les Trois produced this year’s French Derby winner Anabaa Blue.  That is what is commonly called covering all bases.

            However, just because this recent flurry of activity centers around Allegretta, that does not mean that the rest of the Ibidem family does not deserve recognition.  And it is important to note that one of the main reasons that Allegretta stands so tall is that she is inbred to the family via half sisters Aralia and Asterblute.  Further, since she is also inbred to Alchimist (who descends from another branch of this family), her pedigree actually shows four crosses of half sisters Bayreuth x2/Antwort x2. 

            Finally, because Alchimist and Arabis both carry crosses of Ard Patrick, it is noteworthy that Allegretta’s Oleander double adds two crosses of Ard Patrick’s half brother Galtee Moore.  If ever anyone doubted that intensely inbred horses are often the most prepotent, Allegretta is living proof that the best of these animals are often just that.

            One other thing worth mentioning is that Anabaa Blue, the French Derby winner, carries not only all this inbreeding, but also a double of Riverman (via daughters), plus there is also a double of Belle Sauvage via Noble Lassie (dam of Vaguely Noble) and Promised Lady (dam of Lombard).  The (inbreeding) beat goes on.

            Galileo looks far more modern in that he is inbred to Native Dancer via Northern Dancer and Raise A Native.  What is amusing about doubling this fast (and unsound strain) is that his dam Urban Sea, whose Arc was won over very soft turf, was frequently called a plodder, and big name or not his sire, Sadler’s Wells, has produced a plodder or two himself. 

            We suspect that while this Native Dancer double seems to have meshed well in Galileo so far as Europe is concerned, it remains to be seen whether or not it added enough speed to compete successfully in the U. S.  This would be particularly true should Galileo attempt a run over the main course.  This is no Giant’s Causeway.

            In any event, it seems we are unlikely to get any in-depth history on this female clan apart from what has been recently published, so we’re going to go against our own rule and name Ibidem a Reine-de-Course anyhow, along with her descendents Arabis, Aster and Allegretta.  We were thinking out loud several years ago when opining that German blood was one place to look for substance in Europe.  The situation was not all that different than what the Aga Khan encountered when taking on the Boussac stock and mixing  it with his English bloodlines. 

            The Aga Khan proved that the intensely inbred Boussac lines did well when mixed with English and Irish speed which was becoming too brittle.  Now Ibidem’s German descendents may be starting to show they are capable of the same type of melding, for as we’ve noted before, there are few types more inbred than the good German lines.  Allegretta is living proof of that.
