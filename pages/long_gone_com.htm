        As we discussed in Pedlines #88, we think it is quite fitting to give some space to horses bred by Pedlines readers.  Our subject this month, Long Gone Con, was bred by John James Tobin and he also happens to be the result of one of our suggested matings.

         Mr. Tobin first contacted us in 1996 regarding his Zein mare Very Much  The Lady.  The granddaughter of Zeddan had not won, but she had been a surprisingly good producer considering how badly her matings had been planned.  Her best foal had been her first, a colt by P. Vik that had earned $87,981.  Second dam Hello Doily was also bred on the same Irish Castle-Nellie Flag cross as champion Bold Forbes and she was tail-female to Nellie Flag,

so her family was good.

         At that point in time, Memo had been mentioned as a possible mate for Very Much The Lady, and while he has not done badly, we found a better match for the mare in Grade 1 winner Restless Con, a Haskell victor who had also run second in the G2 Ohio Derby.  Restless Con was developed locally, in the San Francisco Bay Area, so we had ample opportunity to see him.  He was a strong, handsome horse with good bone and he had plenty of class.

        The mating would effect a 3 x 4 cross of half siblings Next Move and Sea-Change.  Restless Con also offered a rare son cross (County Delight) of Count Fleet to balance the mare’s Sea-Change line and his Bull Lea blood played right into her Calumet lines of Sun Again/Bull Lea/Nellie Flag (all of which are family 9-F and trace to the foundation mare The Apple).

         Mr. Tobin decided to give the horse a try.  (My letter noted, “Although I know you prefer to stay in Southern California, I strongly recommend you send the mare north to Restless Con”).  In 1998, Very Much The Lady produced a  roan colt that would run as “Long Gone Con”.

         Unfortunately, the little fellow was claimed from Tobin before he got a chance to have much fun with him.  After three unsuccessful tries in maiden allowance company, he was entered for $40,000 and was claimed for that price by J. Terrence Lanni.  In the race itself, he ran second and in his very next start he won.  His win came in his first try around two turns; all his previous tries had been sprints.  His new owners, by the way, also decided to geld him, something that makes Jim Tobin rather sad.  “There was no reason to geld him,” he remembers, “He was easy to handle, not at all studdish or fractious.  Juan Garcia (Tobin’s trainer) said there was no reason to geld, but some want to geld all Cal-breds.”

         Since that time, Long Gone Con has run 14 times.  He’s won four of those races, been second six times, third once, fourth once and thus off the board only twice.  As of this writing, he has won $279,393.  He’s at his best between a mile and 1 1/16 miles and he’s won on turf as easily as dirt. 

         Lanni held on to the gelding for quite a while, but picked up a partner, Bernard Schiappa, along the way.  Long Gone Con ran well for both their trainers, Scott Hansen and Craig Dollase.  They then decided to risk him for a tag and he was claimed for $80,000 in a winning race on June 26 by the partnership of King Arthur Farms, Richmond and Wellman.  Their trainer is Doug O’Neill.  The gelding rewarded his new owners with a sterling second in a straight $68,000 mile turf allowance in his first start for them at Del Mar on July 30.

         Jim Tobin and I often exchange e-mails about the horse and his breeder would like to see him turned into a turf miler specialist, an idea with which we concur.  We’d both especially love to see him run in the California Cup Turf Mile. 

         Whatever path he actually takes, though, the best thing about this little horse is his tick-tock consistency.  He loves to fly out of the gate and control the pace of his races.  Nearly every time he has won he has offered a “game response” according to his charts and if he has not won, he’s lost in a game effort.  He has been an absolute joy to follow and for Tobin, a nice source of Cal-bred breeders’ checks.

         Also, something quite ironic has occurred regarding his dam.  In 1999 Tobin decided to send Very Much The Lady to Three Chimneys in order to breed her to Miesque’s Son, the full brother to Kingmambo which had just arrived in this country.  It was a big decision, and it was going to cost quite a bit of money.  We applauded Tobin’s desire to upgrade his mare and we supported it whole heartedly.  But it was not to be. 

         We’re not going to name any names, but despite a lengthy discussion with a representative of the farm during which we explained the strength of the match, Very Much The Lady was turned down.  Which is where the irony comes in.

         Sometimes if you wait long enough, what you want will come to you and last year Miesque’s Son ended up in California, obviously not deemed quite enough like his full brother for Three Chimneys Farm.  But he’s still quite fine for Very Much The Lady and she has been bred to him.  It will be the ultimate paradox if she produces his best runner to date, and we wouldn’t bet against it. 

         For now, however, we’re just enjoying Long Gone Con himself.  Even in the missteps in his career, like when he was taken to Florida for the Sunshine Millions Classic - a truly stupid move - he has made us proud.  The poor little man broke in the air, was wide on the home turn and tried with all his heart to find the front end where he knew he belonged.  He ran sixth that day, but that placed him right in the middle of the field.  A decent break might have had him in the money at the very least, and he missed a $50,000 check by only three-quarters of a length. 

         We’ve always thought you could tell more about a horse by the way he loses than the way he wins and “Con” told us plenty that day.  We’re awfully tickled with him.
