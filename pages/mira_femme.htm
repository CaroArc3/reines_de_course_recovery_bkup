Another Stellar Member of the 5-F Family

During the past six months a number of crises with which many of our Pedlines readers are familiar with have kept us from getting all the Reine-de-Course work done which we would like to have completed.   Nevertheless, we feel it is time to call attention to the Number 5-F family of La Grisette (1905 by Roi Herode-Miss Flora by Melton).

To date, we have have covered only the Grey Flight branch of the family which descends from La Chica (1930 by Sweep).   But La Grisette has two other important daughters which we intend to cover in due course.   One is Miyako (1935 by John P. Grier), best known as the second dam of Native Dancer and another is Danise M. (1933 by Epinard), the third dam of Mira Femme.

Mira Femme was an extraordinarily quick California-bred filly who was champion of her age and sex in 1966, the year she won the Arlington and Hollywood Lassie Stakes, plus the California Breeders’ Champion, Junior League and Astronaut Stakes and placed in the Lassie Trial Stakes.    She went on at three to add two more stakes, the Santa Ynez and Pasadena Stakes.

As a producer, Mira Femme produced only two stakes winners:  Illinois Derby (G3) winner Batonnier and Femme Elite, a Group 1 winner (Grand Criterium) in France.

Batonnier, who recently passed away, was a tremendously successful sire in California, getting 20 stakes winners including Cavonnier, who narrowly missed in the 1996 Kentucky Derby; California champions Batroyale; and Charm A Gendarme; Charmonnier and G1 winner Batty.   His young son Robannier has sired four minor stakes horses in his first two crops and seems a worthy successor.

Batonnier, a son of His Majesty, was one of several His Majesty horses who helped to make this family truly shine.   Others include champion Tight Spot (exported to Japan for stud) and his full brother, Valiant Nature, a G1 winner now at stud in Kentucky and off to a good start with nine stakes horses in his first three crops.

Then there is Chic and Sassy, dam of G1 winner Valley Crossing, another young horse off to a fine start at stud, with 12 stakes horses from his first four crops.   Valley Crossing stands in Maryland.

Clearly, His Majesty and Mira Femme had a special affinity and we were interested in discovering its source.   Here’s the lowdown on what this mating creates: Three crosses of Sainfoin to a line of his full sister Sierra; a sex-balanced cross of Pharos to a cross of Colorado, who is bred on the same Phalaris/Chaucer cross.   Three are also three lines of Tracery, sex-balanced by sons Papyrus and Obliterate to daughter Teresina; three lines of Mumtaz Mahal via a sex-balanced cross of Mahmoud (Oil Capitol/Boudoir II) plus Furrokh Siyar, and yet another sex-balanced double –this time of Son-in-Law (via Beau Pere/Inkling).

So what we have here is a classic middle-distance stallion and a very fast filly from a very fast family whose pedigrees meld to create almost perfect balance.   The combination produced versatile horses who were capable of doing many different things - from Valiant Nature’s precocious Hollywood Juvenile win to Valley Crossing’s good handicap record.   It is also clear that we needed to address branch of the La Grisette family in order to acknowledge Mira Femme as the sire-source mare she has become.

When we finish the La Grisette family’s story and put all the pieces in place, there will be several new Reines-de-Course, but for the time being, we feel it is important to acknowledge Mira Femme.   Therefore, we are going to go ahead and name her a Reine-de-Course for her contribution of sound speed and the ability of her branch of the family to produce good sires.  This expands the family enough that it is fit and proper to also name La Grisette.

Batonnier’s loss is deeply felt in California.   But he has a good young son (and time to get another) and lots of daughters out there to keep the family line alive and kicking. This is a grand family to inbreed to and remember that when you use any of them with Native Dancer and/or Grey Flight that you are linebreeding to a very fine group of horses.   It’s time now to bring that inbreeding closer in and, say, try a daughter of Batonnier with Valley Crossing.  You’re breeding soundness, speed and versatility x2 when so doing and that seems like a very good idea to us.
