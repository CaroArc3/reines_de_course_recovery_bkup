REVISITING IBIDEM


“King’s Best is bred altogether differently than Bluemamba. He traces to
the German foundation mare Ibidem via the Arabis group of mares whose
family is responsible for a whole slew of German Derby winners: Allgau;
Athenagoras; Alba; Allasch; Asterblute; Ako; Abendfrieden; and Alchimist.
Arabis is certainly worthy of Reine status. It’s gathering the information
on German horses that is holding us up, not any failing on her
part.”                                                    .......PEDLINES, June 2000.


Of all the prophetic things we’ve written in these pages (and we’ve
had our share), we’ve probably never hit the nail on the head quite so
correctly as we did in this June 2000 story on European classic horses.
Since that time, dig as we might, the only information on Ibidem
forthcoming was a list of her descendents and the name of her breeder
(Gestuet Romerhof, breeder also of her dam, Bayreuth).

We put her file aside, but did not forget her, despite one European
friend assuring us that the family was getting weaker not stronger.  You
know who you are, and you get our wet noodle award for that call!

In any event, this year not only is the Ibidem family in the news, it
literally IS the news in Europe.   Pacemaker wrote “Derby Winners
Linked By German Roots”, and The Thoroughbred Times’ excellent
pedigree writer John Sparkman wrote a whole story on Ibidem’s
descendent Allegretta.  Thanks to that story, we can fill in the blank
spots pertaining to one part of Ibidem’s family, though it does not tell
the whole tale.

We already knew that Ibidem had been sold by her breeder to
Freiherr S. A. von Oppenheim, owner of Gestuet Schenderhan.  If was
from this German stud that Allegretta, the subject of The Thoroughbred
Times story, was purchased.

What has caused this flurry of interest in Ibidem’s family (and
most particularly in Allegretta’s sub-branch) is that since Allegretta
herself produced 2000 Guineas winner King’s Best, not one but two of her
daughters has produced a European Derby winner - and both of them
accomplished that feat this year. Allegretta’s Prix de l’Arc de
Triomphe-winning daughter Urban Sea has out the English and Irish
Derby winner Galileo and Allegretta’s stakes winning Riverman
daughter Allez Les Trois produced this year’s French Derby winner
Anabaa Blue. That is what is commonly called covering all bases.

However, just because this recent flurry of activity centers around
Allegretta, that does not mean that the rest of the Ibidem family does not
deserve recognition.  And it is important to note that one of the main
reasons that Allegretta stands so tall is that she is inbred to the family
via half sisters Aralia and Asterblute.  Further, since she is also inbred to
Alchimist (who descends from another branch of this family), her
pedigree actually shows four crosses of half sisters Bayreuth x2/Antwort
x2.

Finally, because Alchimist and Arabis both carry crosses of Ard
Patrick, it is noteworthy that Allegretta’s Oleander double adds two
crosses of Ard Patrick’s half brother Galtee Moore.  If ever anyone
doubted that intensely inbred horses are often the most prepotent,
Allegretta is living proof that the best of these animals are often just that.

One other thing worth mentioning is that Anabaa Blue, the French
Derby winner, carries not only all this inbreeding, but also a double of
Riverman (via daughters), plus there is also a double of Belle Sauvage
via Noble Lassie (dam of Vaguely Noble) and Promised Lady (dam of
Lombard).  The (inbreeding) beat goes on.

Galileo looks far more modern in that he is inbred to Native Dancer via Northern Dancer and Raise A Native.  What is amusing about doubling this fast (and unsound strain) is that his dam Urban Sea, whose Arc was won over very soft turf, was frequently called a plodder, and big name or not his sire, Sadler’s Wells, has produced a plodder or two himself.

We suspect that while this Native Dancer double seems to have
meshed well in Galileo so far as Europe is concerned, it remains to be
seen whether or not it added enough speed to compete successfully in
the U. S. This would be particularly true should Galileo attempt a run
over the main course. This is no Giant’s Causeway.

In any event, it seems we are unlikely to get any in-depth history
on this female clan apart from what has been recently published, so
we’re going to go against our own rule and name Ibidem a
Reine-de-Course anyhow, along with her descendents Arabis, Aster and
Allegretta.  We were thinking out loud several years ago when opining
that German blood was one place to look for substance in Europe. The
situation was not all that different than what the Aga Khan encountered
when taking on the Boussac stock and mixing it with his English
bloodlines.

The Aga Khan proved that the intensely inbred Boussac lines did
well when mixed with English and Irish speed which was becoming too
brittle.  Now Ibidem’s German descendents may be starting to show they
are capable of the same type of melding, for as we’ve noted before, there
are few types more inbred than the good German lines.  Allegretta is
living proof of that.

 

REINES ADD POULE D’ESSAI DES

POULAINS TO CLASSIC SUCCESS

Reines-de-Course added another chapter to their burgeoning 2001 classic success with the victory of Godolphin’s Noverre in the May 13 Poule D’Essai Des Poulains (French Two Thousand Guineas).

Noverre, who hails from the family of Reine-de-Course Lady Comfey (profiled in PEDLINES #50, Feb 2000) is of particular interest as he is a three-quarter brother to champion Arazi, sire of this year’s American classic hopeful Congaree.

In point of fact, Noverre has an even better pedigree than Arazi owing to his sire, Rahy’s, cross of Halo. This gives Noverre a double of Almahmoud (Cosmah/Natalma). Both brothers share a sex-balanced cross of stamina influence Wild Risk (Runaway Bride/Le Fabuleux) and a cross of Spring Run/Tom Fool (which contains sex-balanced Menow and a *Bull Dog daughter double).

Both are inbred to Nearco, but Noverre has a better Lady Josephine contribution via an extra *Mahmoud cross (Cohoes) and a cross of *Royal Charger (via Hail To Reason/Halo). The Cohoes cross also sex-balances his daughter double of *Mahmoud via Almahmoud x2.

We see only one major danger in doubling these brothers somewhere down the road and that is the four crosses of Wild Risk such a coupling would effect. Wild Risk x4 may be exactly what the modern Thoroughbred does not need; it is simply too stout.

REINES IN THE CLASSICS

Reines-de-Course swept the top three spots in the Kentucky Oaks, One Thousand and Two Thousand Guineas, and also accounted for Kentucky Derby winner Monarchos.

In the Oaks, winner Flute traces to the Sister Sarah branch of Pretty Polly; runnerup Real Cozzy to *Boudoir II and third-place finisher Collect Call to the Sanfara sub-branch of Maggie B. B.

In the Two Thousand Guineas, both winner Golan (IRE) and Tamberlaine (IRE) are descendents of Reine-de-Course Country House, she a descendent of Reine-de-Course Simon’s Shoes. Third place finisher Frenchman’s Bay (FR) traces to the Evening Mist sub-branch of Maggie B. B.

One Thousand Guineas winner Ameerat (GB) is a direct descendent of the “flying filly”, Mumtaz Mahal via Reine-de-Course Malindi, a full sister to *Nasrullah. Her closest rival, Muwaklel (GB) is a descendent of Reine Colosseum and Toroca, the third place finisher, traces to the Reine Molly Adare, herself a descendent of the mighty Pretty Polly.

Despite the wonderful performance of the top three finishers in these major events, the most gratifying win for the Reines-de-Course on this historical weekend was the electrifying win by Monarchos in the Kentucky Derby. Monarchos’ pedigree was profiled in the March issue of Pedlines, which means we wrote him up well before he won the Florida Derby.  Horses with pedigrees this good seldom escape our notice.

Monarchos not only traces to Reine-de-Course Golden Trail; his dam is inbred to another great Reine, *Forteresse, via full sisters Delta Queen and Rarelea. It’s a most unusual pedigree and his sire is a lovely horse who might have worn the roses himself had he not been wasted at two.

Given how this horse is bred, not to mention how marvelously he was handled, there have been few more worthy Derby winners in recent memory.

And no matter what happens from now on, this is a horse we won’t have to worry about. People who treat their horse with care prior to the Derby are highly unlikely to wreck him after he’s won the thing.

Reine-de-Course Families Earned Five Eclipse Awards In 2000

Horses descending from Reine-de-Course families won five 2000 Eclipse Awards, including Horse of the Year.

Tiznow, champion three-year-old and Horse of the Year descends from the great Chilian speed source Reine-de-Course *Papila.  This is the family of Crimson Satan and Tiznow is also, of course, a full brother to $2 million earner Budroyale.

Surfside, the latest champion for Triple Crown winner Seattle Slew, descends from the Reine-de-Course family of Furlough and is also out of Eclipse Award-winning filly Flanders.  This family has also produced champion Mrs. Penny and Northern Jove, sire of champion two-year-old filly Candy Eclair.

Lemon Drop Kid, champion older horse, is the latest in a long line of champions and classic winners descending from Reine-de-Course *Uvira II.  Others to call this family their taproot are champion and classic winner A. P. Indy, classic winner and sire Summer Squall and champion sire Raja Baba.

Kona Gold, unanimous choice for champion sprinter, is a descendent of the great Reine-de-Course Grey Flight, dam herself of nine stakes winners.

This family has also produced champions Successor, Bold Lad (USA), Inside Information, and Cozzene's Prince as well as leading sires What A Pleasure and Sovereign Dancer.

The Speed of *Papila Rules The Classic

Tail-female descendent Tiznow and *Papila relation Giant's Causeway ran one-two in the Breeders' Cup Classic, stamping this great Chilean Reine-de-Course once again as one of the main sources of quality speed in the stud book.

Tiznow's fifth dam is Blue Canary, a daughter of *Papila, while Giant's Causeway gets his *Papila cross via Crimson Satan (a *Papila son) in the pedigree of his sire, Storm Cat. Tiznow is also, of course, a full brother to Budroyale, second in last year's Breeders' Cup Classic and a major winner this year as well.

Other Reine-de-Course descendents winning Breeders' Cup events were Sprint winner Kona Gold, whose fourth dam is Reine-de-Course Grey Flight and Distaff victress Spain, who traces to Reine-de-Course Bourtai.

Runners-up who traced to Reines were Surfside (Furlough) and Honest Lady (Teresina). Intriguingly enough, both are daughters of Seattle Slew.

Several Reines-de-Course were also represented by sires and broodmare sires of winners. Sprint winner Kona Gold is by Java Gold, a descendent of Reine-de-Course Home By Dark; Juvenile Fillies winner Caressing is by Honour And Glory, a descendent of Reine-de-Course Marguerite de Valois.  Seattle Song (a descendent of Reine-de-Course Maggie B. B.) is the broodmare sire of Tiznow; Slew o' Gold (a Bourtai descendent) is the broodmare sire of Kona Gold; Regal And Royal (a descendent of recently named Reine-de-Course Beaver Street) is the broodmare sire of Spain; Green Dancer (a descendent of Reine-de-Course Sly Pola) is the broodmare sire of Turf winner Kalaniski; Our Lassie descendent Blushing Groom is the broodmare sire of Juvenile winner Macho Uno and Caressing is out of a mare by Majestic Prince who traces to Reine-de-Course *Boudoir II.

So far as the number of Reines-de-Course in winners' pedigrees, Kona Gold is "richest" with 30 Reine presences, while both Caressing and Kalinski have 25 Reines-de-Course in their six crosses.  Spain has 21 Reines in her pedigree; Perfect Sting 20; Tiznow 18; and Macho Uno and War Chant 16.
