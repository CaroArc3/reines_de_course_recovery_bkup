The Reine-de-Course series now numbers over 400 mares in 65 family groupings.  As one can imagine the written history of so many mares, along with pedigree and family tables, is an enormous amount of information.

For one person to have access to the entire series they would have had to follow it from its inception, first in Owner-Breeder, then later in The Homestretch magazine.  It is unlikely that anyone could collect all of the articles today.  Alternatively, the series is regularly reprinted in our own newsletter, Pedlines, but since that publication started several years after the creation of the Reine-de-Course series, articles on numerous Reines have yet to appear there.  Those reprints, while updated when they appear, will always be several years behind the original appearance.

It is our feeling that nowhere does so much historical information about so many important mares exist in one place.  In an effort to consolidate the information so that it is available as one publication we decided to create The Reine Books, a group of three ring binders utilizing our original Pedlines articles, but again updating them and including pictures that never appeared in the newsletter.  Once all of the Pedlines articles have been included we will bring the series current by adding the Reines who have yet to be reprinted in Pedlines.  At present this wealth of information will require four 1" binders.

The three ring binder approach offers us the ability to send out individual page updates for easy insertion so that the material never becomes outdated.  Readers can rearrange the articles in whatever order they prefer (family numbers, alphabetical, whatever).  And it offers us the economy of printing a book as ordered as opposed to having a bunch of bound books lying around that can't be readily updated.

Unlike other books and articles about female families, the Reine series discusses the pedigree of the main mare in depth and oftentimes suggests ways to inbreed to her family in order to strengthen the bloodline.  Therefore, this series is of more practical use to breeders than most historical information currently available.

Each article has been updated for this presentation, and a sample of an article can be found under "Our Newest Reine" on this site.  Additionally each article includes a six generation pedigree of the Reine the article centers on (Ron is still trying to figure out how to get a six generation pedigree on the website without going blind).
