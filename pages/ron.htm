Breeding Court Vision

By Ron Parker

The retirement of Court Vision to stud elicited the following observation from trainer Dale Romans:

"People should know that this is the type of horse who can right the ship a little bit in the soundness of racehorses.  When you get a horse that's raced as long as he has, he can put his soundness and durability back into his foals."

Do we agree with that observation?

Yes, up to a point.  Court Vision's sire, Gulch (Mr. Prospector--Jameela, by Rambunctious) is generally regarded as a sound sire, primarily because the unsound aspects of a single strain of Mr. Prospector appears to be nullified by the sound qualities Jameela offers.

His dam, Weekend Storm (Storm Bird--Weekend Surprise, by Secretariat) presents us with the unsound aspects of Storm Bird, but again there are the sounder qualities Reine-de-Course Weekend Surprise (Family 3-L, Reine-de-Course *Uvira II) brings to the pedigree.

What breeders have to be careful of is to avoid injecting more unsound strains when matching mares to Court Vision, and in particular avoid adding more Mr. Prospector or Storm Bird to the mix.

Intelligent mating as opposed to creating more unsound "commercial" foals just to get big bucks at a sale is what's needed if there is any hope of reversing the unsound course Thoroughbred breeding has taken.

Of course it's easy to express sweeping generalizations and in the interest of delving into specific ways to accomplish this, Ellen offers some suggestions.

Look for A.P. Indy or Raja Baba-line mares that are absent Mr. Prospector/Raise a Native and Storm Bird.  Plus look for mares with *Uvira II lines like Skip Away, Plugged Nickle, or Slew City Slew that are similarly absent these crosses.

As a new sire, Court Vision offers the chance to do right by the resultant foals by blending soundness to soundness right out of the gate.

November 2011

Musings

Ron Parker

 

November 4

 

I FORGET HIS NAME but who was that obnoxious clown on the Breeders' Cup telecast that flatly declared that "anyone who doesn't vote for Groupie Doll [in Eclipse voting] should be thrown off the commission."

Well, it's not a commission, it's a couple of hundred individuals in three voting blocs, and while Groupie Doll will no doubt be an overwhelming  choice for Female Sprinter, we certainly have a problem with a talking head who pontificates a personal opinion in the form of excommunicating anyone whose free voting rights don't agree with their opinion.

I am reminded of an incident last year surrounding the Eclipse voting.  The National Turf Writers and Broadcasters, one of the three voting blocs, publicizes all of their individual voters selections in all of the Eclipse categories. 

Now there are some critics of this openness who feel a voter's selections are a private matter and should remain private and retained as confidential information by the independent firm that tallies the Eclipse voting.

But since the NTWAB put it out there for all the world to see, one of that organization's officers went through the selections, voter by voter, and where he didn't feel a particular selection fit what he felt to be an appropriate choice he sent emails to those voters questioning not only their selection but also their credentials, creating by interpretation a veiled inference that if their credentials didn't meet what he felt made them qualified to vote then perhaps they could be tossed out of the NTWAB and thus denied Eclipse voting privileges.

Give some people a television gig (wonder what his credentials are, especially since he thinks the voting is done by commission) or a list of who voted for whom and they revel in telling people what they should do or what they did wrong.

 

WE WONDER if the folks at Lane's End happened to see a letter from Mike O'Toole of Scranton, Pennsylvania that appeared in the Blood-Horse issue dated 11/3/2012:

O'Toole sent a photo of him up close and personal with Rachel Alexandra.

"This photo was taken at Stonestreet Farm on 'See Rachel Day' Oct. 12," O'Toole wrote.  "When I went up to Rachel Alexandra with my Rachel hat and sweatshirt and turned to get my photo taken she grabbed onto my shirt as if to say, 'This is my shirt with my blaze and name on it.'  The folks at Stonestreet Farm really pulled out the red carpet for us Rachel fans."

 

October 20

 

WE DON'T KNOW if our comment about Lane's End (see below) had any bearing on a bloodhorse.com announcement on October 18 that the farm will host a "Fan Appreciation Day" on November 4 (10 AM to 2 PM), but it sure is coincidental.

Or maybe so many fans expressed their displeasure that Will Farish decided to try and stop the bleeding.

Now the announcement did say that "at other times, breeders' can inspect stallions by making an appointment" and we don't know if that means that "Fan Appreciation Day" is a non-appointment day to see the stallions or if it's really a day for the fans since no mention is made of the planned activities.

That no mention includes whether Zenyatta will be available so it will be interesting to see just what it is Lane's End is doing to "appreciate" the fans.

 

I SEE HOLLYWOOD PARK is "chugging along" several years after it was announced the developers would tear it down for 238 acres of "nearly 3,000 dwelling units, 620,000 square feet of retail development, 75,000 square feet of office and commercial space, a 300-room hotel and 25 acres of open or recreational space."

You'd think California might have learned its lesson when Bay Meadows in Northern California was reduced to a pile of rubble and replaced by a "development" so a bunch of people could enrich their bank accounts.

But no, the developers are waiting in the wings looking for financing for the project while Hollywood Park can't commit to racing dates next Fall because they don't know when the bulldozers will show up.

Why a coalition of California racing interests can't buy the place and preserve it is beyond me, but the City of Inglewood is frothing at the bit to put hundreds of people in the unemployment office and presumably increase their tax revenue in the name of progress.

Tradition and history go out the window because what the world really needs is another Safeway.

 

WHERE DID I READ recently that an investigation into some ten or twelve Thoroughbreds that were injured at a track indicated they had all previously raced at another track that was not identified.

The speculation that Oaklawn Park was the culprit.

Now I read that Oaklawn Park has hired one Kevin Seymour as track superintendent to lead them into their 2013 meeting.

No mention is made of what happened to the previous track superintendent.

Do the dots connect or what?

 

LOVE THOSE SHOW PRICES when a heavy favorite doesn't hit the board, don't you?

The Grey Stakes (G3) at Woodbine on October 7 only had a field of six with Uncaptured sent off at 2-to-5 odds.

Uncaptured finished fifth, triggering Show prices of $18.00, $14.50 and $45.20.

Yes, I've seen larger returns in such a situation, but not that I recall when there were only six runners.

 

OWNER MIKE REPOLE announced that he would not send any horses to this year's Breeders' Cup because of the new requirement that two-year-olds in the five juvenile races race without Salix, aka Lasix, a ban that will extend to all Breeders' Cup races next year.

"If you want to experiment with racing two-year-olds without Lasix, I think I'm open to that," Repole said.  "But let's do it on the second race at Belmont on a Wednesday afternoon.  Let's not do it on one of the biggest days when people are actually paying attention.  To me it makes no sense.  Why would you want to experiment with top two-year-olds?  I wouldn't want it to be my horse who bleeds or gets injured.

"I always say you need to worry about the horse first and the fans second.  This decision seems to say they're not really worried about the horse and they're not really worried about the fans."

The purpose of Lasix is of course primarily to prevent bleeding.

So while a ban on most medications is an admirable goal, a ban on Lasix doesn't make a lot of sense unless you clear up the air quality around tracks in congested areas.

Yes, let's ban Lasix and start with this year's Breeders' Cup at Santa Anita Park in airy Los Angeles.

What did you say the number was on today's Smog Alert?

October 5

BLOODHORSE.COM reports that Stay Thirsty is being pointed for one more race.

Trainer Todd Pletcher indicated that the four-year-old Bernardini colt would run in either the Breeders' Cup Dirt Mile (G1), Breeders' Cup Classic (G1), or the Cigar Mile (G1).

Bloodhorse.com also stated in the article that "Stay Thirsty was retired with a record of 4-5-1 from 16 starts and earnings of $1,726,000."

Huh?

PENNY CHENERY was honored by the Thoroughbred Club in Lexington on September 30.

Among her remarks (bold face mine):  "It is our job as owners to promote the horse.  If you are lucky enough to have a good horse, make that horse available to the world; create a website, send out e-mails.  If you love the industry, promote it."

Penny also noted that on the previous day she signed 180 autographs at the Bourbon County festival in Paris, Kentucky.  "And yesterday 300 tourists went through Claiborne Farm.  That is what we have to do to keep the industry going."

 

I WONDER if anyone at Lane's End Farm was paying attention since that's the current home of Zenyatta, whose racing exploits attracted national exposure and thousands of mesmerized fans.

Oh, Zenyatta has a website, that's a plus even though I have a problem with her first person, uh first horse, commentary.

She was available to the world when she was racing but once they stashed her away at Lane's End about the only way a fan could see her would be to parachute into her paddock and hope you get a few pats in before they haul you off to stony lonesome for about 300 years.

Lane's End might as well put up signs at the entrance that read "Tourists Will Be Shot On Sight."

 

KEENELAND is hosting a "Racehorse Summit" on October 16 and 17 and the list of topics to be discussed cover injury prevention and the injury database, safety and "trainer education," pre-race inspections, aftercare programs, corticosteroids, and "Selected Effects of Training and Racing on the Musculoskeletal System."

Missing from this formidable array of topics seems to be an commentary on unsound breeding, the foundation of some of the problems.

Well, we certainly wouldn't want to admit in an open-to-the-public summit that we have been steadily weakening the Thoroughbred through poor breeding choices prompted by big sales dollars and to hell with what happens to the horse later.

Do that and TOBA members would lead the charge for the exits.

Well, you can check out a live feed of all the other stuff at either keeneland.com or grayson-jockeyclub.org on those dates, we don't denigrate the value of some of the topics, only that there is a lot of head in the sand in this industry.

Besides, I really am curious about that "trainer education," I know a few that could use some help.

 

KEENELAND is also tinkering with one of racing's most sacred traditions, the $2 bet to Win, Place, or Show.

For their current meet they are offering $1 Win, Place, and Show wagers.  And why not in an age where you can bet as little as a dime in exotic pools?

I think it's a good idea, there have been days at the track when I was down to my last dollar but couldn't get a Win bet on what I knew was a sure thing at 30-to-1 in the next race because of the $2 minimum.

Of course I envision some bettor getting shut out at a window because some little old lady is in front of him unwadding $1 bills while he/she reflects on the good old days when tracks had denomination windows and it wasn't crowded at the $50 window.

But hey, a $1 bet has the potential to compete with lotteries.

This crossed my mind the other evening when we were having dinner at an Applebee's and several television sets were tuned into one of the horse racing channels.

What an ideal situation to have a betting machine so someone waiting for their meal could invest a buck because they saw a "pretty horsey."

That kind of exposure and the ability to participate is how you get new fans.

 

DR. LARRY BRAMLAGE (DVM, MS, Diplomate ACVS) was recently interviewed by the Paulick Report on "the impact of corrective surgeries on foals and yearlings" and he wasn't too kind about what's happening to the breed these days, noting that "we'll breed to anything that produces a fast horse."

"I'm one of those people who believe we really have changed the conformation of the breed--but not by the surgical conformation," Dr. Bramlage said.  "It;s that we have moved the entire breed to a different conformation; the offset knee.  It's happened because those have been the most productive horses.  It's the stallion that traces back to certain individuals that are prone to do that, and I don't know if I dare name them for publication."

 

THE BREEDERS' CUP keeps trying to come up with new ideas to promote the sport, and I don't just mean the Breeders' Cup Cupcakes (designed by Goodie Girls Bakery in the Breeders' Cup colors) that will be for sale at $4 a pop at this year's Breeders; Cup events.

At almost the end of the baseball season they came up with a "Breeders' Cup Night" at Dodgers Stadium, with jockey Chantal Sutherland throwing out the first pitch and Dodger fans getting to meet jockeys and trainers and view in-game programming of Breeders' Cup highlights and coming events.

I doubt that it will lure all those Dodger fans to Santa Anita but it is exposure, and that's a good thing, let Los Angelenos know there's something in life besides the infield fly rule.

I do think Craig Fravel, Breeders' Cup President and Chief Executive Officer, went a bit overboard when he was quoted as saying that "we're honored to be a part of the Dodgers' final homestand in what has been an incredibly exciting season for the club."

I'm not sure what's "incredibly exciting" about finishing the season eight games back of the division winner and historic arch rival Giants and not even getting into the playoffs as a wild card, but I guess you get your thrills where you can find them.
