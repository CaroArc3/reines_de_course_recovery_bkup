Home Page

Tell Me When He’s Won It!

Ellen Parker

In any horse person’s lifetime there are a handful of horses that touch you in such a way that is impossible to explain. For us, Round Table and Dahlia were human, and almost human were Swale, Mill Reef, Swaps, and Seattle Slew.

Then one autumn day in 1993 we were watching Belmont races on television and a colt caught our eye. We never used to like greys, always preferring plain bays like Round Table, but Holy Bull had swagger, courage, and a heart that kept showing itself as he grew from a dark juvenile into a brilliant white champion.

Holy Bull won that day in the Belmont Futurity, and the rest of his story never stopped feeling personal. We remember the power he carried at Keeneland, the races that made us hide in the guest bath rather than watch live, and the long afterlife of his courage through Confessional, Macho Uno, and Giacomo.

Holy Bull was a horse we loved so much we could not watch him run. That is saying a great deal, and it is why this 2017 homepage opened with a memorial reflection rather than another navigation tree.

Triple Crown Reflections

Ellen Parker

And Then There Were Three

By June 2017 the Triple Crown season had narrowed to three classic winners, with Tapwrit emerging as the colt whose pedigree most deserved serious attention. The homepage reflection tied his Belmont win to Forest Song, Seattle Slew, Round Table, Monarchy, and the larger history of the female families that defined the site.

Tapwrit was presented not as a one-race curiosity but as another example of how the archive reads pedigrees through structure, balance, and deep family memory rather than fashion. That same piece also paused to note how much this crop still had left to prove.

Cloud Computing - The Second Chapter in a Very Strange Year

The companion reflection turned to Cloud Computing, Classic Empire, and the unsettled shape of the 2017 classics. It weighed softness and strength in the pedigree, watched for the influence of Seattle Slew, In Reality, Friar’s Carse, and Humanity, and treated the spring not as settled truth but as an unfinished argument.

Together these two pieces are what the 2017 home page actually foregrounded: current essays and pedigree judgment, with the larger family archive waiting in the left column.
