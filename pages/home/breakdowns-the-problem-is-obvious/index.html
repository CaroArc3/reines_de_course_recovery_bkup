About This Site

Ellen Parker, who Leon Rasmussen once dubbed as “Round Table’s Boswell”, has been a pedigree analyst and turf writer for most of her adult life.  She created the Reine-de-Course (“Queens of the Turf) to recognize the influence of female families when no such recognition existed at the time.

Ron Parker has been writing about Thoroughbreds–both seriously and humorously– in various publications for half a century.

Both are long-time members of the National Turf Writers and Broadcasters organization.

Breakdowns – The Problem Is Obvious

 

Ellen Parker

Recent developments, specifically the deaths of 30 horses at Santa Anita, have managed to enrage not just the PETA people but has gone all the way to the U. S. Senate.  In said Senate, California’s senior Senator, Diane Feinstein seems to want to shut down an industry that employs many of her constituents…..but that’s a subject for another day.  Besides, she’s not alone, one just wonders if there aren’t other matters that should be occupying a Senator’s time.

To beat a dead horse if you’ll pardon a perfectly dreadful pun, we need to be clear here:  You have to ask more questions about why these horses have broken down and when you do that you have to look at the breed as a whole.  Going that far, however, hits the big commercial breeders where it hurts because many of their highly commercial sires are the culprits.

Breeders today don’t seem to care about bone and they sure don’t care about how many starts a horse has so long as he manages to get some graded black type.  (The gold standard in this old lady’s day used to be if he won $100,000, now it’s G1 and over $1 million).  What everyone wants is a catalogue page that is riddled with graded black type.

There is nothing wrong with this per se.  However, when the goal in breeding is that and only that – not keeping say a gelding from the family at the track for 50 starts – then we lose the toughness that made horses like Kelso and Forego and John Henry such superstars.  Instead we rush them off to make more light-boned horses in not one but two hemispheres before we can find out how sound they are.  In other words, one no longer gambles on whether a stallion will be a good sire alone, they also gamble on whether he can get his offspring to the track, let alone stay there long enough to make all the training money worthwhile.

Today as we have said too many times to count, the bloodline of the Thoroughbred has narrowed to basically Mr. Prospector and sons and various branches of Northern Dancer, with a huge emphasis on Sadler’s Wells and Danehill because of Coolmore and shuttling.  (First they tried to outcross with Germans, thus ‘polluting’ that gene pool, now they are using Deep Impact and starting to pollute the Japanese lines).  The bottom line is that everyone is running out of ways to outcross because the stud book – and there is nothing wrong with this – has been closed for over 300 years.  But what is wrong is that no effort has ever been made to preserve lines that appear not less competent or classy but merely less commercial.

So we are down to Include for Domino and Mucho Macho Man for Plaudit, Round Table and *Gallant Man are basically gone.  Seattle Slew is held up by A.P. Indy, who we love, but try to find one free of Northern Dancer or Mr. Prospector.  Then there is Man o’ War who lives on via Relaunch and a bit of Successful Appeal and there are bits and pieces like Monsun, Ahonoora, etc. in Europe and a handful of Star Kingdom in Australia.  Some of this is not even tail-male.

If one counts the loss of our personal favorite Round Table, it is not rare to find him inbred to, but unless one of the Gunpowder-bred sons of K One King carries on, he is finished.  How we wonder how he might have fared had he gone to South America or somewhere in Europe where he would have been appreciated as an outcross.

But things have changed – everyone wants the quick buck and they want it as quickly as they want their cell phone to call up a pedigree.  It’s a new world, preppie, another words and that’s not necessarily a good thing for the way we breed our horses.

So Santa Anita gets too much rain and, asked to do something very difficult like keep the track in decent shape, what do we get?  Breakdowns.  We get breakdowns because in many cases because our horses are hothouse flowers.  These are not mustangs we have out there; they are fragile now and if they are fragile maybe, just maybe they actually need medications of varying types to train and run.

There are few people in the world who love horses more than the two who occupy this house.  But we have seen horses get hurt despite the best care in the world in our lifetime, watched them fall, break legs and other bones and we still love the sport.  The only difference between a football player with a broken leg and a horse is that nobody is going to shoot the football player.  All athletes run the risk of injury, but humans aren’t going to be destroyed because they are not commercial in the sales ring!

That means the humans around the horse have to take responsibility for his care – and if necessary make the sometimes difficult decision to end his suffering.  However – and this is the single most important thing we can ever emphasize – we hate seeing breeders start a horse out with the handicap of being nothing more than a pretty catalogue page.  Thus at the end of the day Santa Anita can close when it rains, ban drugs, rule off Hall of Fame trainers or whatever, but it still all starts at the farm.

When the breeder sits down to plan his mares’ matings for the coming year, if he does not think about the future, if he does not consider that he is creating a living being that will be asked to race about 40 mph around turns with a man on his back, then it simply doesn’t matter.  We can think of no better way to work this whole thing than lifetime ownership – a ‘forever home’ for every horse, meaning in this case that what you breed you own:  If your horse won’t stand training, you bring him home and care for him until he either dies a natural death or becomes too ill not to put down.

If a good owner comes along that wants to repurpose him – say make him a hunter or a dressage horse that is fine.  But there will have to be a catch – the horse can only be sold once.  If he can’t do what the second owner wants – say he’s sloppy over fences when they thought he’d soar – then back home he goes again.  That should shutdown the stock yards in a hurry.

Not long after we once moved, we decided we might like to move back to our former home.  But we could not take all four cats with us.  So we stayed put.   When our house caught on fire recently, we would not leave them on the premises – nor would we leave them overnight in smoke-polluted air – until they were safely crated and able to go with us.  PETA should give us a badge.

Perhaps that is an extreme example, but when one takes on the responsibility of – or breeds a living creature – he should be responsible for a lifetime.  These horses give their lives for our pleasure as Debbie McAnally said when Go For Wand went down in her battle with Bayakoa.  Who is to say they don’t understand what that might mean if they could talk?  Meaning we have to talk for them.

So we are saying – from the horse’s mouth as it were – please breed us with thoughtfulness.  Give us time to mature and grow into our best years and treat us as the individuals that we are.  If it is not our lot in life to become a G1 winning two-year-old, give us enough time to be a top older allowance winner, don’t drop us down into the cheapest ranks with a small injury so that that injury becomes a big one.  And most of all, you breeders and owners – these are no less your children than the ones who live in the house with you.  You made them happen; now take care of them or pay the price – even Washington D. C. is watching.
